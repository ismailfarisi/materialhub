# Unit Economics, Revenue Models and Operating Costs: B2B Materials Marketplace in the UAE (as of Nov 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-08. Method: 15 web searches and fetches. stripe.com could not be reached from this session (the host is not in the network allowlist), so Stripe figures come from secondary sources. Most cost data in this space is published by consultancies and vendor blogs, not primary sources, so every figure is labelled with its source and how reliable it is. Items under "Inferences" are my own estimates or calculations, not sourced facts.

FX assumptions used in my estimates: AED 3.6725 = USD 1 (the peg); roughly INR 23-24 per AED (approximate, not sourced here).

## 1. Take rates and revenue models (global and MENA), and distributor gross margins

### Takeaway
Product marketplaces usually take 5-20% of GMV. B2B sits at the low end because order values are larger. Faire (15% plus a new-customer fee) and Amazon Business (subscription plus 8-20% referral fees) are the reference points for commission models. IndiaMART shows that a subscription or lead-gen model works for fragmented suppliers, at roughly ₹63-66k (about AED 2,700-2,850) a year per paying supplier. Paper and packaging distributors run at about 24% gross margin (Veritiv), which limits how much commission a reseller-type vendor can absorb to the high single digits.

### Cited Findings
- **Faire:** 15% base commission on wholesale orders. For non-North-American brands, the first order with a new retailer adds a 10% new-retailer fee, about 25% in total, then 15% on reorders, plus 1.9-3.5% payment processing — [eightx.co](https://eightx.co/blog/faire-wholesale-take-rate-margin-impact). Another source describes a flat $10 new-customer fee instead, with an effective first-order cost of about 19% including processing — [wholesalehelper.io](https://wholesalehelper.io/blog/selling-on-faire-vs-having-your-own-store/). 0% commission on "Faire Direct" orders from a brand's own existing accounts — same sources. Sacra reports Faire at about $616M/yr revenue, growing 74% — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/faire-b2b-retail-marketplace-growing/) (date of the estimate not confirmed).
- **Amazon Business (seller side):** $39.99/month Professional seller plan plus referral fees of 8-20% by category; enterprise buyer-side plans reach $2,999.95/month — [ecommercefastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/10-b2b-marketplaces-for-finding-buyers-in-2025/), [wizcommerce](https://wizcommerce.com/blog/top-b2b-marketplaces/). These are secondary sources; check Amazon's fee page.
- **General benchmarks:** product marketplaces are typically 5-20%; because B2B orders are much larger, take rates tend to be lower. Take rate = revenue / GMV, and the headline rate gets diluted by processing fees, refunds and operations — [Origami Marketplace](https://origami-marketplace.com/en-gb/marketplace-take-rate-a-guide-for-marketplace-operators/), [Applico](https://www.applicoinc.com/?p=13202).
- **Mirakl:** does not publish pricing. It is enterprise SaaS for operators, not a take-rate benchmark — [wizcommerce](https://wizcommerce.com/blog/top-b2b-marketplaces/).
- **IndiaMART (subscription and lead-gen model, FY25 to Mar 2025):** collections about ₹1,626 crore (+10% YoY) — [GuruFocus transcript](https://www.gurufocus.com/stock/NSE:INDIAMART/transcripts/2813895). Q4 FY25 consolidated revenue ₹355 crore (+13%) and FY25 net profit about ₹181 crore — [Entrepreneur India](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/business-news/indiamart-closes-fy25-with-a-net-profit-of-inr-181-crore/490851). Paying subscribers about 2.17 lakh (217k) and ARPU about ₹65,590 (+11%) — [mydomicile substack](https://mydomicile.substack.com/p/indiamart-fy25-the-cash-machine-built). Q3 FY25: 214k paying subscribers, ARPU about ₹63,000, and a net loss of 3,715 suppliers in the quarter (churn among low-tier plans) — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-earnings-call-indiamart-loses-3715-suppliers-q3fy25/). The sources do not fully agree on subscriber counts and ARPU; the primary source is the NSE/BSE filings.
- **Tradeling (Dubai, B2B marketplace):** Crunchbase calls it MENA's "dominant" B2B e-marketplace — [Crunchbase](https://crunchbase.com/organization/tradeling). Caplight shows headcount growing from 244 to 282 (+16% QoQ) at a June 2025 data point — [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/tradeling) (no context given; unverified). Earlier profile: about 400 suppliers from more than 25 countries — [ANBA](https://anba.com.br/en/menas-b2b-marketplace-looking-to-expand-to-latin-america). No public commission rate found.
- **Distributor gross margin (paper and packaging):** Veritiv (the largest US paper and packaging distributor) has a gross margin of about 24.3% (3-year median 23.8%; MRQ 24.8%). TTM to Sep 2023: revenue $6.09bn, gross profit $1.48bn — [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stock/nyse/vrtv), [Alphaspread](https://www.alphaspread.com/security/lse/0lp2/profitability/ratio/gross-margin). Packaging grew from 34% (2016) to 41% (2018) of Veritiv net sales — [Veritiv 10-K FY2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1599489/000159948919000003/vrtv-20181231x10k.htm). Veritiv was taken private in 2023, so these are the latest public figures.

### Inferences
- A vendor on MaterialHub who is a distributor or trader earning about 20-25% gross margin can realistically bear an **all-in 6-10% commission** (including payment processing) before switching to off-platform deals. Converters and manufacturers (rigid boxes, printed cartons) probably earn higher gross margins (my guess is 25-40%; not sourced) and can bear more.
- Recommended hybrid revenue model (my estimate, modelled on Faire, Amazon and IndiaMART):
  - **Commission** of 6-8% on B2B trade orders and 12-15% on B2C craft and retail (consumer marketplaces in the region generally charge more; not sourced here).
  - **Supplier subscription** for featured listings, RFQ access and analytics, priced at AED 250-1,000/month (IndiaMART's ARPU of about AED 2,700-2,850/yr is a floor for what SMEs will pay for leads).
  - **RFQ and lead fees** for large runs where the transaction may go off-platform.
  - Embedded finance and BNPL revenue share later.
- Leakage risk: large repeat B2B orders tend to move off-platform. A 0% "bring your own customer" lane like Faire Direct, plus subscription revenue, protects against this.

### Gaps
- No published commission rates for Tradeling, noon Business, Amazon.ae Business, or MENA packaging-specific platforms.
- Alibaba.com Gold Supplier pricing (historically about $3-6k/yr by tier) could not be verified this session.
- No Antalis or Gulf-region distributor margins found; MENA paper/packaging distributor margins are unknown.
- No a16z/NFX take-rate essay was fetched.

## 2. Payment costs in the UAE (Stripe, Tabby, Tamara, COD) and tax

### Takeaway
Card payments cost about **2.9% + AED 1** domestically on Stripe UAE (secondary sources; there are conflicting reports of 3.045% + AED 1.05). International cards add about 1%, and currency conversion adds another 1%. BNPL costs the merchant about **4-8%** (most often cited as 5-6%), so it only makes sense for B2C or small orders. COD carries a courier surcharge (about AED 3-10 plus 1.5-3.3% of the cash collected) and a **20-35% return-to-origin (RTO)** rate for many UAE merchants.

### Cited Findings
- **Stripe UAE:** 2.9% + AED 1 per successful domestic card charge; +1% for international cards; +1% for currency conversion; foreign card with conversion = 4.9% + AED 1 — [Digital Nexa](https://blog.digitalnexa.com/how-much-does-stripe-charge-for-credit-card-payments-in-the-uae), [checkoutjoy](https://checkoutjoy.com/countries/uae/), [Ziina](https://ziina.com/blog/stripe-alternatives-with-cost-effective-transaction-fees). One source reports 3.045% + AED 1.05 domestic and 1.05% surcharges — [globalfeecalculator](https://globalfeecalculator.com/?p=1214) (unverified; possibly a newer rate). Stripe confirms it offered UAE early-adopter promotional pricing at launch (2021) and that the +1% currency-conversion fee is unchanged — [Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/uae-promotional-pricing-faqs). Product availability in the UAE — [Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/which-payments-methods-and-products-are-available-in-the-uae).
- **Tabby (merchant fee):** reported at 5-8% per transaction by category and volume — [checkoutflow](https://checkoutflow.pages.dev/ae-en/best-payment-gateway-ecommerce/tamara-vs-tabby); about 5-6% citing Tabby merchant documentation — [checkoutflow seller guide](https://checkoutflow.pages.dev/ae-en/best-payment-gateway-ecommerce/tabby-guide-sellers); 6-7% plus a fixed fee — [hidubai](https://focus.hidubai.com/how-to-offer-installment-payments-in-dubai-without-an-e-commerce-website/). Free for consumers; late fees apply — [Tabby](https://compliant.tabby.ai/l/en/how-tabby-works/are-there-any-fees-on-using-tabby).
- **Tamara (merchant fee):** about 4-8% — [checkoutflow](https://checkoutflow.pages.dev/ae-en/best-payment-gateway-ecommerce/tamara-vs-tabby); KSA sources cite 3-6% for both — [Sendan calculator](https://www.sendantech.com/en-SA/tools/payment-gateway-fee-calculator). All BNPL figures are low-confidence; rate cards are negotiated.
- **COD courier fees:** Aramex COD surcharge cited as 3.3% of COD value (older rate sheet) or AED 3-5 + 1.5%; Fetchr AED 5-7 + 2%; iMile AED 4-6 + 1.5%; typical add-on AED 5-10 or 5-15 per shipment — [sellershipment](https://sellershipment.pages.dev/ae-en/best-courier-ecommerce/aramex-ecommerce-guide), [sellershipment UAE overview](https://sellershipment.pages.dev/ae-en/best-courier-ecommerce/best-courier-service-uae).
- **COD failure and RTO:** 20-35% RTO for many UAE merchants; a realistic target is <10% for a mixed COD and prepaid merchant; prepaid-first merchants reach 3-5% — [Quiqup](https://www.quiqup.com/post/how-to-reduce-rto-rate-uae-ecommerce). COD-heavy fashion and electronics: 20-40% returns vs single digits for prepaid — [iqfulfillment](https://www.iqfulfillment.com/?p=6136). India comparison: 25-30% COD RTO vs 2-3% prepaid — [Dazeinfo](https://dazeinfo.com/2024/06/05/over-25-of-cod-orders-fail-a-major-dent-in-indias-e-commerce-business). These are vendor sources; no official statistic found.

### Inferences
- **Blended payment cost estimate (mine):** about 2.0-2.5% of B2B GMV if most of it is cards and bank transfer (bank transfer is near-zero cost and common for trade orders), or about 3.5-4.5% of B2C GMV with a 25-35% BNPL mix.
- **Policy:** limit COD to small B2C orders (for example under AED 500), and require a prepaid deposit or card for B2B. A failed COD delivery of bulky goods costs forward plus return shipping (about AED 30-100+ for heavy cartons) with no revenue.
- **Tax (background knowledge, not verified this session; confirm with the FTA and MoF):**
  - VAT is 5%, with mandatory registration above AED 375k of taxable supplies.
  - Commission charged to UAE vendors is a taxable supply.
  - Federal corporate tax is 9% on taxable income above AED 375k (0% below). Small Business Relief applies for revenue up to AED 3m for tax periods ending by 31 Dec 2026.
  - Free-zone Qualifying Free Zone Person 0% treatment generally does not cover mainland B2C sales.
  - Stripe Connect payouts and dispute fees were not verified.

### Gaps
- Official Stripe UAE pricing (stripe.com blocked); Stripe Connect fees for UAE platforms (Connect availability for UAE platforms needs confirmation); chargeback fee.
- Official Tabby and Tamara merchant rate cards (not public). Tabby and Tamara business or B2B products (e.g. "Tabby for Business") not researched.
- Alternative acquirers (Checkout.com, Network International, Telr, PayTabs) were not benchmarked and are often cheaper at volume.

## 3. Logistics costs in the UAE for heavy and bulky goods

### Takeaway
Small parcels are cheap: AED 6-22 per parcel up to 1 kg, depending on volume tier. Same-day delivery in Dubai costs AED 20-60. However, sourced data on **pallet, roll and multi-carton freight between emirates and across the GCC is essentially missing**, and needs carrier quotes.

### Cited Findings
- **Aramex domestic:**
  - Walk-in: AED 15-22 up to 1 kg.
  - Contract: AED 10-15 for low-volume accounts, AED 6-9 for high-volume accounts.
  - 0.5-1 kg: AED 18-22 walk-in vs AED 6-9 enterprise.
  - Under 0.5 kg contract: AED 8-11.
  - Sources: [sellershipment Aramex guide](https://sellershipment.pages.dev/ae-en/best-courier-ecommerce/aramex-ecommerce-guide), [sellershipment UAE](https://sellershipment.pages.dev/ae-en/best-courier-ecommerce/best-courier-service-uae).
- **Fetchr:** AED 14-18 per 1 kg; same-day in Dubai AED 20-25. **iMile:** AED 8-12 per 1 kg contract (cheapest) — same sources.
- **Same-day in Dubai:** AED 35-60 per parcel (2026 comparison) vs AED 20-40 for 4-6 hour delivery (2026 guide) — [uaefreezonefinder](https://uaefreezonefinder.com/uae-courier-last-mile-delivery-guide-2026/), [samvertex](https://samvertex-web.pages.dev/blog/same-day-vs-next-day-uae/). Quiqup estimates operators spend about AED 22-27 per same-day run (an operator cost, not a seller price) — [Quiqup](https://www.quiqup.com/post/same-day-document-courier-uae-costs-rules-tech).
- Cross-border GCC shipping guide (no reliable figures extracted) — [sellershipment GCC](https://sellershipment.pages.dev/ae-en/ecommerce-shipping-cost/shipping-from-uae-to-gcc). FedEx publishes a UAE domestic rate PDF — [FedEx](https://www.fedex.com/content/dam/fedex/international/rates/fedex-rates-domestic-ar-ae.pdf) (not parsed).

### Inferences
- **Estimates, not sourced:**
  - Heavy cartons (10-30 kg) via courier, with per-kg surcharges: roughly AED 25-80 within the UAE.
  - Pallets and rolls of board or leather need LTL or pickup-truck freight. Typical 1-3 tonne pickups within Dubai or Sharjah are likely a few hundred AED per trip; Dubai to Abu Dhabi costs more. Get quotes from local truck aggregators.
- **Model implication:** let vendors ship bulky B2B orders themselves (vendor-fulfilled, with freight charged to the buyer at cost). Use platform courier contracts only for samples, swatches and B2C parcels, which supports the roadmap item for discounted sample shipping. A sample pack under 1 kg at AED 8-12 contract cost is affordable to subsidise.
- **GCC cross-border:** expect customs duty (the GCC common external tariff is typically 5% for non-GCC origin goods; goods of GCC origin with a certificate are generally exempt), plus destination VAT (KSA 15%) and broker fees. This is background knowledge and needs verification.

### Gaps
- Pallet, LTL and FTL rates between emirates; GCC cross-border freight and customs clearance costs; Aramex freight (non-express) rate card; Quiqup and Shipa published rates. None were found in reliable sources.

### Inferences

At about AED 20k/month loaded, that is about **AED 700-1,300 per activated supplier**, plus catalogue photography of maybe AED 500-2,000 per supplier.

## 5. Cost to build and run (team, hosting, licence, office, visas, burn)

### Takeaway
A free-zone licence starts at about **AED 12,500-12,900** with zero visas (Meydan, IFZA) and costs about AED 17.7-21.4k with one visa. First-year realistic costs are AED 25-31.5k. DMCC costs AED 35-53k+ in year one. UAE software engineer pay ranges widely, from about AED 112k/yr base (Payscale) to AED 344k median total compensation (Levels.fyi, which skews towards big tech). Offshore development is typically much cheaper (not sourced this session).

### Cited Findings
- **IFZA:** AED 12,900 with no visa; AED 17,700 with one visa; AED 24,600 (or AED 24,250 in another source) with two visas — [avyanco IFZA](https://avyanco.com/ifza-free-zone-cost/), [businessdubai IFZA vs Meydan](https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/ifza-vs-meydan-free-zone).
- **Meydan:** AED 12,500 with no visa; AED 20,670-21,050 with one visa; AED 27,600 with two visas — same sources plus [avyanco Meydan](https://avyanco.com/meydan-free-zone-cost/). Outlier: "around AED 18,000 basic" — [DIAC](https://diac.ae/blog/meydan-free-zone/).
- A headline IFZA setup of AED 12,900 really costs **AED 25,000-31,500 in year one** once office, establishment cards, visas and insurance are included — [bcl.ae](https://bcl.ae/blogs/dubai-free-zone-trade-licence-cost.md).
- **DMCC:** from about USD 17,800, with a mandatory office and stronger banking access — [businessdubai](https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/ifza-vs-meydan-free-zone); AED 35,000-53,000+ in year one including a flexi-desk — [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/feeds/blog/dmcc-company-formation-cost-uae).
- First visa costs about AED 8,500 at most Dubai zones (conflicts with IFZA's lower increment above) — [bcl.ae](https://bcl.ae/?p=536466).
- **UAE software engineer pay:** Payscale average base about AED 112,403/yr — [Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/AE/Job=Software_Engineer/Salary). Levels.fyi median total compensation AED 343,979 (p25 AED 240k, p75 AED 450k) — [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/en-gb/t/software-engineer/locations/united-arab-emirates?city=6764). A guide gives a range of AED 180k-900k — [CareerCheck](https://www.careercheck.io/blog/software-engineer-salary-dubai-2026).

### Inferences
- **Licence:** a marketplace that sells to mainland UAE buyers and holds vendor funds may need a mainland (DET) licence, or a free-zone licence with the right e-commerce or portal activity. Payment flows should go through a licensed PSP (Stripe Connect or similar) so the platform does not hold funds itself; holding funds could trigger CBUAE stored-value or payment rules. This is background knowledge and needs a legal check. Budget AED 25-50k for licence and setup in year one.
- **Engineering (my estimates):**
  - Senior full-stack engineer in Dubai: about AED 25-40k/month loaded.
  - Offshore equivalents: India or Pakistan about AED 6-15k/month; Egypt about AED 5-12k/month.
  - An offshore or hybrid team of 3-5 engineers costs about AED 40-80k/month, vs AED 120-200k/month all in Dubai.
  - The existing codebase (one ElysiaJS process, Postgres/PostGIS, Redis, BullMQ, Typesense) is lean.
- **Hosting (estimate):** about USD 500-1,500/month (AED 1.8-5.5k) at seed scale for managed Postgres, Redis, Typesense, a container host, object storage, CDN, email and SMS.
- **Visas:** about AED 4.8-8.5k per visa (sourced above), plus mandatory health insurance of about AED 1-5k per person per year (estimate).
- **Office:** a flexi-desk is often included in the free-zone package. A small serviced office in Dubai costs about AED 3-10k/month (estimate).
- **Typical seed burn (estimate):** a seed-stage MENA B2B marketplace with a 10-20 person team burns about USD 80-200k/month (AED 0.3-0.75M). No MAGNiTT benchmark was found.

### Gaps
- Official free-zone fee schedules (only consultancy blogs found); DET e-Trader and mainland e-commerce licence fees; Hays and Cooper Fitch tech salary tables; offshore salary surveys for India, Pakistan and Egypt; MAGNiTT burn and team-size benchmarks.

## 6. AOV, order frequency and break-even GMV (worked example)

### Takeaway
No sourced AOV or order-frequency benchmark for SME packaging buyers in the UAE was found. Under the stated assumptions, a lean team (about AED 210k/month fixed costs) needs about **AED 3.2-4.7M of monthly GMV (AED 39-56M/yr)** at an 8-10% take rate to break even on contribution, and about AED 14M/month at a 5% take rate.

### Cited Findings
- B2B average order sizes are much larger than in B2C, which pushes take rates down — [Origami](https://origami-marketplace.com/en-gb/marketplace-take-rate-a-guide-for-marketplace-operators/).
- IndiaMART supplier ARPU of about ₹63-66k/yr is a reference for subscription revenue per supplier — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-earnings-call-indiamart-loses-3715-suppliers-q3fy25/), [mydomicile](https://mydomicile.substack.com/p/indiamart-fy25-the-cash-machine-built).

### Inferences (worked example; all assumptions are mine)

**Assumptions**
- B2B AOV AED 2,500. An SME box maker or printer buying a mix of board, paper and fittings, ordering about 1.5 times a month.
- B2C craft AOV AED 180.
- GMV mix: 90% B2B, 10% B2C.
- Payment cost: 2.0% of GMV blended. Platform absorbs it from the commission; B2B is mostly card and bank transfer.
- Other variable costs: 1.5% of GMV. Covers customer support, sample shipping subsidy, COD RTO losses, chargebacks and refunds.
- Contribution margin = take rate − 3.5%.

**Fixed costs (AED per month, estimate)**

| Item | AED/month |
|---|---:|
| 4 offshore engineers | 50k |
| CEO + ops/category lead (Dubai) | 50k |
| 3 field sales / supplier onboarding (loaded) | 54k |
| 1 customer support / ops | 9k |
| Marketing (search, samples, content) | 35k |
| Cloud and tools | 6k |
| Licence, visas (amortised) and office | 6k |
| **Total** | **≈ 210k (≈ AED 2.5M/yr, ≈ USD 690k/yr)** |

**Break-even GMV**

| Take rate | Contribution % | GMV/month to break even | GMV/year | B2B orders/month (AOV AED 2.5k) | Active buyers (1.5 orders/month) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5% | 1.5% | AED 14.0M | AED 168M | ~5,000 | ~3,400 |
| 8% | 4.5% | AED 4.67M | AED 56M | ~1,680 | ~1,120 |
| 10% | 6.5% | AED 3.23M | AED 39M | ~1,160 | ~780 |
| 12% | 8.5% | AED 2.47M | AED 30M | ~890 | ~590 |

**Subscription upside:** 200 suppliers at AED 500/month adds AED 100k/month. This roughly halves the GMV needed (at 8%, break-even drops to about AED 2.4M/month).

**Sensitivity:** if the platform also subsidises delivery, for example AED 40 per B2B order, that is 1.6% of a AED 2,500 AOV. Variable cost rises to about 5.1%, and an 8% take rate then needs about AED 7.2M/month.

**Conclusion:** a pure 5% commission model is not viable at seed scale. A blended 8-10% effective take, combining commission with subscriptions and sample or RFQ fees, is needed.

### Gaps
- Real AOV and frequency data for UAE packaging buyers (would require supplier interviews or Tradeling and noon Business disclosures); the size of the UAE packaging materials market; buyer retention curves.

## 7. MENA seed and Series A funding environment for B2B commerce (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
MENA venture funding reached a record in 2025 (Wamda: about $7.5bn). B2B startups raised about $2.8bn, ahead of B2C. The UAE had a record $1.4bn in 9M 2025 (+188% YoY, MAGNiTT). E-commerce is a smaller category (about $372.5M in 2025). Consolidation among inventory-heavy B2B players (Sary merging with ShopUp into SILQ) suggests investors favour asset-light models with real unit economics.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 MENA startup funding was a record of about $7.5bn; B2B startups raised $2.8bn, outpacing B2C; e-commerce raised $372.5M, behind fintech and proptech — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2026/01/record-year-mena-startups-funding-climbs-7-5-billion-n-2025).
- Saudi Arabia and the UAE together raised about $3.13bn in 2025 (MAGNiTT) — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/business/investment/saudi-uae-startups-led-vc-deals-raised-313bln-in-2025-ugdijcpx). Saudi Arabia raised $1.72bn across a record 257 deals — [Scene Now](https://scenenow.com/Business/Saudi-Arabia-Leads-Middle-East-Venture-Funding-With-1-72B-in-2025).
- UAE: a record $1.4bn in 9M 2025, +188% YoY, driven by foreign capital (MAGNiTT) — [Enterprise AM](https://enterpriseam.com/uae/2025/10/16/uae-venture-funding-up-188-in-9m-amid-foreign-capital-surge/). Middle East VC raised a record $2.77bn in 9M 2025 — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2619206). MENA funding over 5 years was $15.4bn, with corporate investors backing 12% of deals — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2643406).
- July 2025: B2C startups captured $534M, reversing the earlier B2B lead — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2612029). February 2026: e-commerce attracted $52M across 3 deals, mostly Breadfast's $50M — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-leads-menas-start-up-funding-despite-february-dip).
- Sary (KSA B2B marketplace) merging with ShopUp (Bangladesh) to form SILQ Group (April 2025) — [Arab News PK](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2596842). Retailo history: $36M Series A; acquired DXBUY — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-news/retailo-raises-36-million-in-series-a-round-for-empowering-community-commerce-in-mena-1.1643979896995), [Martechvibe](https://martechvibe.com/?p=17928). In 2021, B2B startups raised $1.5bn, 52% of the $2.9bn MENA total — [Forbes ME via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/uae/forbes-middle-east/20220301/282836489565038).
- A vendor blog claims "$3.8B across 688 deals in 2025", which conflicts with Wamda's $7.5bn total; the difference is likely methodology, such as excluding debt or mega-rounds — [Pubrio](https://pubrio.com/blog/best-b2b-prospecting-tools-mena-2026/) (low confidence).

### Inferences
- Headline totals are inflated by mega-rounds and debt (fintech and AI); seed-stage B2B commerce rounds in the UAE are typically about $1-5M (my estimate).
- Investors are cautious about B2B models with heavy inventory or credit (retail grocery B2B consolidation: Sary to SILQ, Retailo's pivots). The pitch for MaterialHub should be asset-light (vendor-fulfilled, no inventory), take-rate positive from the first order, with embedded finance (trade credit via partners) as later upside.
- No evidence was found of Tradeling layoffs or a down-round. Caplight even shows headcount growth in 2025. The idea of "investor sentiment after Tradeling" is unconfirmed.
